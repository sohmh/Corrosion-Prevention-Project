Corrosion is an **electrochemical process** where a refined metal is spontaneously converted into a more chemically stable form, such as an oxide, hydroxide, or sulfide, essentially reversing the metallurgical extraction process. **Rusting**, the corrosion of iron, is the most common example.

The process of corrosion requires four fundamental components working together, forming a **micro-electrochemical cell**.

## 1. The Essential Components (The Corrosion Cell)

Corrosion cannot occur without these four elements:

1.  **Anode (Oxidation):** The site where the metal is **oxidized (loses electrons)** and dissolves into the electrolyte. This is where the damage occurs.
2.  **Cathode (Reduction):** The site where a chemical species (usually oxygen or hydrogen ions) **gains the electrons** released by the anode. This site is protected from corrosion.
3.  **Electrolyte:** A conductive medium (like water, moist soil, or humid air) that allows **ions to move** between the anode and cathode, completing the circuit.
4.  **Metallic Path (External Circuit):** The metal itself, which allows the released **electrons to flow** from the anode to the cathode.

---

## 2. The Electrochemical Reactions

Corrosion proceeds in three simultaneous stages:

### A. Anodic Reaction (Metal Dissolution)

At the anode, the metal atoms lose electrons and become metal ions, dissolving into the electrolyte.

$$\text{M} \rightarrow \text{M}^{n+} + n\text{e}^-$$

**Example (Iron/Steel):**
$$\text{Fe} \rightarrow \text{Fe}^{2+} + 2\text{e}^-$$

### B. Cathodic Reaction (Electron Consumption)

The electrons ($\text{e}^-$) flow through the metal to the cathode where they are consumed by a reduction reaction. The specific cathodic reaction depends on the environment:

| Environment | Cathodic Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Neutral or Alkaline ($\text{pH} > 7$) Aerated Water** | Oxygen Reduction: $$\mathbf{\text{O}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} + 4\text{e}^- \rightarrow 4\text{OH}^-}$$ |
| **Deaerated/Acidic Water ($\text{pH} < 7$)** | Hydrogen Evolution: $$\mathbf{2\text{H}^+ + 2\text{e}^- \rightarrow \text{H}_2 \uparrow}$$ |

### C. Formation of Corrosion Product (Rust)

The metal ions ($\text{Fe}^{2+}$) formed at the anode and the hydroxyl ions ($\text{OH}^-$) formed at the cathode migrate through the electrolyte and react to form the visible corrosion product.

**Example (Rusting of Iron):**

1.  **Initial Reaction:** $\text{Fe}^{2+} + 2\text{OH}^- \rightarrow \text{Fe}(\text{OH})_2$ (Ferrous Hydroxide)
2.  **Oxidation:** Ferrous hydroxide is unstable in the presence of oxygen and further oxidizes:
    $$4\text{Fe}(\text{OH})_2 + \text{O}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow 4\text{Fe}(\text{OH})_3$$
3.  **Final Product (Rust):** $\text{Fe}(\text{OH})_3$ loses water to become **Rust**, which has the general formula $\mathbf{\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3 \cdot x\text{H}_2\text{O}}$.

This corrosion product is typically porous, flakes off, and exposes fresh metal underneath, allowing the corrosion cycle to continue.
